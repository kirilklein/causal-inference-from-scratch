# Exercises: What if?

Answer before opening the [solutions](solutions.md).

1. In one sentence, name this lesson's population, treatment contrast, outcome, and follow-up time.
2. The script reports a true effect of +12 and an observed difference of −8.27. Must one calculation be wrong? Explain the two comparisons.
3. A patient's health rises from 40 before treatment to 55 afterward. Does that establish an effect of +15? Specify the missing comparison.
4. A training program improves three workers' potential earnings by 0, 10, and 20 units. What is the average effect? Does it describe every worker's effect?
5. Replace the treated indices with `{0, 1, 2, 3, 4}`. Predict the direction of the change in the observed difference, run the script, and explain why the simulator ATE stays fixed.

Keep a four-sentence explanation: the causal question, the unobserved information, the observed comparison, and why the two comparisons can differ.
